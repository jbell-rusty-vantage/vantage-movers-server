## Highest-priority logic defect found: repeated equal Call move dates

`loadGranotLeadProjection` includes `move_date` for Form Leads (`processor.ts:2074`) but omits it in the Call Lead return (`processor.ts:2098–2138`). `leadDesiredState.ts:383` plans an accepted Call move date independently of broad enrichment. Its semantic diff therefore compares the incoming date to `undefined` on every fresh dated Call snapshot, even if Mongo already holds the identical date. `synchronizeLeadFromGranot.ts:328` revalidates allowed paths/receiver authority but does not redo that semantic comparison. `buildLeadUpdate` treats those planned paths as reportable (`:591`), sets move provenance, and `:203` requests Sheet Sync for any reportable path other than priority. The path also confirms the Record Link and records `lead_updated`.

A pure local planner reproduction, using synthetic values and no DB/environment loading, produced:

| Projection supplied for an unchanged dated Call | Outcome | Changed paths | Advances temporal winner |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current projection, missing `move_date` | `applied` | `move_date` | yes |
| Complete projection, equal persisted `move_date` | `already_current` | none | yes |

This is a concrete source defect and a demonstrated planner consequence. Live `evidence/logic-probes.json` confirms 17,512 Call Lead EntityChanges from `synchronizeLeadFromGranot` across New York November 14–15 whose only changed paths are `current_move_provenance` and `last_accepted_granot_observation`: no business date value changed. The same window has 17,624 applied Decisions requesting Sheet Sync. These measured path-only writes are the dominant applied-write class; do not label them real customer changes. Reproduce the pure planner proof with `node --import tsx docs/capacity-review-2026-11-16/evidence/move-date-planner-repro.cjs`; recorded output is `evidence/move-date-planner-repro.json`. The script asserts synthetic planner behavior; actual loader omission is established by source inspection, and actual production consequence by the aggregate path evidence. It is not a full replica integration test.

Put a fix for this defect before a semantic compaction redesign: populate the canonical Call projection's `move_date`, centralize projection completeness to prevent reader/transaction drift, and verify same-value acceptance advances ordering without a business revision, EntityChange, Record Link confirmation effect or Sheet intent. Retain the normal path for a genuinely changed date and preserve stale rejection. Add an end-to-end replica regression through **the actual projection loader**, not only a planner fixture with a hand-complete Lead. Do not erase the already-recorded history of these writes; current retention excludes effect-bearing chains. Quantify the newly avoided fan-out/bytes over representative slots after release.

## 1. What creates the volume

`src/services/granotHttpCollector/estimatesCron.ts:2` defines 18 nominal New York slots per day: 00, 02, 04, 06, every hour 08–20, and 22. The trailing 90 inclusive provider Open Dates are partitioned into seven-day windows (`estimatesDateWindow`, `estimatesDateChunks`), with older verified provider-date coverage added by the schedule. The base is 13 windows × 18 slots = 234 child scans/day before older dates and Pool work. DST and incomplete/held slots mean this is a nominal schedule, not a daily observed count.

The November 5 release intentionally retained fresh immutable evidence even for equal values. Its release handoff explicitly leaves sustained capacity unqualified. Each approved action has operation identity `${run_id}:${action_id}`. A retry of that identity reuses evidence; a different scheduled run is a distinct observation even when the row is equal. `automation-apply.md` and `capture.md` make this distinction explicit.

`processor.ts:305` loads/upserts the Observation, prepares a decision and compares accepted time; `processor.ts:402–430` performs the metadata temporal CAS and Decision insert in one transaction for accepted business no-ops. `processor.ts:2161` advances the Lead's accepted tuple. `drainer.ts:453–465` finalizes the Receipt, stores the replay result and schedules eligible no-effect automation reviews seven days plus one minute after completion. Thus a no-op still inserts three evidence rows, updates work state, and can advance a Lead's accepted tuple. It does not require an EntityChange or Sheet write.

The prior brief reports 229,324 automation snapshot receipts across two days and 6,156 jobs. That is approximately 114,662 chains/day and 37.25 observations/job over two days. The corresponding prior average logical row sizes total roughly 4.5 KB/chain, or roughly 516 MB/day of uncompressed documents before indexes. This is **not** the measured physical disk slope: compression, reusable pages, index pages, other collections and oplog are separate. The reported approximately 250 MB/day Atlas disk increase must be reconciled using the live inventory.

Do not attribute all `sales_intelligence_jobs` to these no-ops. The post-commit wake runs on each Lead result (`processor.ts:238`), but `numberActivity/leadLinkJobs.ts:189` first finds EntityChanges for that observation and only enqueues from those changes. An accepted no-op has no such change. Sibling mirroring can do additional reads/repairs; job stage and dedupe-key-prefix distributions are necessary to attribute the reported approximately 124,000 jobs/day.

## 2. Current retention contract and capacity limits

| Mechanism | Current implementation | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Admission | Both `createdAt` and `captured_at >= new_data_from` | Future mode cannot purge chains outside that fixed cutoff. Historical cleanup remains separate. |
| Age | Receipt capture, completion, Observation capture and Decision time all older than seven days | A delayed completion extends retention; seven days from capture alone is insufficient. |
| Eligible chains | Exactly one live Decision; already-current or specifically older-than-temporal-winner stale; no effects; completed HTTP automation; valid priority Lead snapshot; no webhook route or Booking action | Webhooks, effects, ambiguous/unmatched work and official outcome evidence are not bulk-expiry candidates. |
| Current evidence | Must have a newer accepted winner and no protected references | Latest winner and provenance/case/Pool/subject/message/EntityChange references remain hot. |
| Replay | Exact closed-operation receipt/hash/kind/observation/decision/outcome/effects/target equality | Run expiry alone is not sufficient proof for chain expiry. |
| Archive | Gzip BSON with SHA-256, upload precondition, read-back, catalog CAS | Lossless archive is the prerequisite to expiry. |
| Final check | Re-read source and protected references in transaction, compare exact archived evidence | Archive publication does not authorize deletion by itself. |
| TTL | Sparse `retention_purge_at` indexes, expireAfterSeconds 0; marks only after proof | TTL does not select records. The engine selects and certifies them. |
| Worker | One 10-minute lease, one page of 64 per invocation, cron every two minutes | Absolute optimistic ceiling 46,080 reviews/day; actual qualified expirations are lower. |
| Shadow | Archive and count qualified chains but keep them in Mongo; revisit next day | Shadow measurements include repeated reviews, not unique chains; shadow can generate archive traffic without freeing Mongo. |

Sources: `retentionEligibility.ts:5`, `futureRetention.ts:100–155`, `futureRetention.ts:196–324`, `retentionReferences.ts:18`, `supersededObservations.ts:5`, `ops/granot-retention-install.ts`, `vercel.json:75`.

At the prior 115,000 incoming chains/day, 64 × 720 = 46,080 is already short by at least 68,920/day, even with zero failures and every review yielding a deletion. Increasing the page size alone is not an adequate fix: the network and transaction stages scale differently, and each large page reads protected references twice. In shadow, re-review traffic also consumes this ceiling. Measure **unique eligible chains removed/day**, not `reviewed` or `archived_reviews`, against arriving eligible chains/day.

The promotion gate is stricter than “seven days since installation”: seven complete UTC days after both arming and working-set maturity, each with at least 100 qualified reviews, three Pool comparisons, no failures, no mismatches and zero TTL stamps (`shadowWeek.ts`; `futureRetention.ts:111`). Pool comparison can only increment when the selected active Pool Lead has evidence version 2 (`futureRetention.ts:245–292`). Missing comparisons can therefore block promotion even with healthy archive processing. Repeated `qualified` counts do not prove unique backlog coverage.

## 3. Corrections to the earlier removal sequence

### Automation runs and closed-run evidence

New runs already have `purge_at = createdAt + 7 days` (`runWorkflow.ts:413`), and the model declares `granot_run_retention_ttl` on that field (`GranotAutomationRun.ts:65`). Verify this index and due-row ages live before treating the 632 MB run collection as an unbounded leak. Its large plans can be a seven-day rotating working set.

`prepareClosedRun.ts:12` intentionally returns `run_still_present` while an automation run row exists. After expiry it reconstructs the operation ledger from the retained receipts and Decisions, checks every completion is older than seven days, enforces at most 5,000 operations and publishes a verified closed-run object. The online worker calls it lazily (`futureRetention.ts:169`). Therefore:

- “0 of 300 present runs has a closed record” is not by itself a defect; the function refuses those runs by design.
- Deleting a source run does not *inherently* make its retained chains permanently unpurgeable. Current closure explicitly expects absence of the source run and reconstructs from retained evidence.
- The brief's instruction “publish closed-run records before deleting runs using the existing operator” cannot be executed as written. The existing function refuses that sequence. Do not manually delete runs during this planning mission.
- `ops/granot-archive-run-closure.ts:16` examines runs found in only the first 32 archive catalog rows. It is a pilot tool, not a complete/backlog closure sweep.

Recommended stronger replacement: close a terminal, lease-free, checksum-locked run **before** assigning its purge date. Verify every selected action's settled result, persist immutable versioned closure with plan checksum and full operation-set fingerprint, re-read under an ownership/CAS fence, then permit source-run expiry. Keep the after-expiry reconstruction path for legacy runs. This requires a schema/runtime migration and replay tests; it is a proposed contract, not existing functionality. Pending-match, failed technical work and recoverable runs must not get a purge date merely because they are seven days old.

### Historical backlog

Partition the live backlog into: before-cutoff historical chains; eligible mature future chains; under-seven-day working set; protected current winners/references; unfinished/invalid/outcome-excluded work; replay-unclosable runs. Publish count and logical bytes for every partition. Only the second group is served by future promotion. Historical deletion requires the existing archive/dry-run manifest workflow plus fresh checks and separate authorization. No blanket claim that L3 removes the entire 1.83M is supported by the code.

## 4. Failed batches: what is known and what is not

The current catch block increments only `failures` and a special Pool `mismatches` counter (`futureRetention.ts:333–349`), then rethrows. It does not persist a bounded failure stage, code, duration or retry count. Aggregate counters cannot reveal whether the prior five failing days represent timeouts, archive availability, lease loss, data integrity or Pool parity.

Concrete possible failure points, all requiring live log correlation before asserting a cause:

1. Every protected-reference collection is queried using a multi-path `$or`, each with `maxTimeMS: 3000` (`retentionReferences.ts:67–74`). The same checks repeat inside the transaction. Missing indexes or large unindexed collections can time out. An `$or` needs suitable coverage on all relevant branches to avoid broad scans; inspect explain plans before building indexes indiscriminately.
2. Up to 64 different job/month groups archive serially. Each may read an old object, upload a new object and read it back twice. `archiveObjects.ts` permits 15-second reads and 30-second writes; the fixed lease is ten minutes with no renewal. At enough network delays, the final transaction rejects `Retention lease changed`, even if uploads succeeded. A platform timeout can leave the lease until expiry.
3. The final transaction contains all per-chain stamps, reference scans and (in shadow) a live-vs-history Pool read. Increasing the page size amplifies transaction duration and conflicts. No evidence currently proves a larger page fits the live transaction budget.
4. Archive equality/hash/identity corruption, a chain too large for 8 MiB decoded BSON, monthly page limit (128 × 512 chains), five catalog-CAS retries, or incomplete closed-run evidence can reject/hold work. Some are safe exclusions, some are thrown errors; expose them separately.
5. A failed page is initially deferred for 30 minutes, a successful but unqualified/shadow page for one day (`futureRetention.ts:152`, `:301`). There is no persisted eligibility-reason distribution, so “reviewed” cannot identify the bottleneck.

Required instrumentation: bounded labels for `select`, `load`, `reference_precheck`, `winner_precheck`, `close_run`, `archive_read`, `archive_write`, `verify`, `transaction`, `pool_compare`; duration buckets; error class/code only; examined/eligible/held-by-reason/unique-archived/stamped/observed-TTL-removals; oldest due age; active lease age; bytes read/written; object operations. Do not place payloads, contact fields or secrets in diagnostics. Live logs remain needed to explain the existing failures.

The fresh index inventory confirms a concrete access-path gap: `entity_changes` has 177,373 rows and no indexes on any of the three provenance paths in the retention `$or`. The parent's `retention_reference_explain` using a synthetic absent evidence ID confirms `COLLSCAN`, 177,376 documents examined, zero keys and 2,328 ms execution, close to the worker's 3,000 ms cap. This proves an expensive current access path, but unavailable historical failure logs mean it does not prove the exact cause of the recorded failed batches. Leads, Record Links, Pool work and subjects also lack many queried reference-path indexes. Prioritize the largest reference collection rather than adding a separate index for every path to small collections. A longer-term compact evidence-reference ledger or indexed flattened reference array is possible only if its writes and removals are transactionally correct and backfill completeness is proven.

### Additional measured query shapes and code write amplification

Atlas advisor evidence in `evidence/summary.json` reports a `granot_automation_runs` shape averaging 10,380 ms across 72 samples. The suggested `{status, last_attempt_at, createdAt, request_snapshot.waiting_for_all}` index corresponds to the worker's recoverable `$or` filter (`runWorkflow.ts:1447`) and global least-recent-attempt claim sort (`:1678`). Test the actual filter and sort on production-like queued/planning/applying distributions. Do not break fairness or lease eligibility by simplifying it into an arbitrary oldest status queue. Compare the candidate index, a bounded eligibility projection, and current indexes using docs examined, keys examined, sort memory and write cost. A separate createdAt suggestion had only two samples and may reflect an operator query; that is not sufficient basis for a permanent index.

The observed `granot_observations` shape (281 ms average, 297 samples) with `priority.valid`, job, capture time and `priority.raw` maps to `salesOutreach/subjects/store.ts:290`, `priorityUncertainLeads`: look for invalid, non-null raw Priority by requested jobs, sorted newest and bounded by `MAX_OBSERVATIONS`. It is not evidence that the retention Pool parity read alone causes the shape. Rehearse a narrow partial index for invalid-priority observations keyed by job and capture time, versus the advisor's broad index; verify Mongo can select it for the full predicate and preserve limit semantics. Broadly indexing every 115k/day normal Observation for a rare-invalid search can be avoidable write cost.

There is also avoidable application write work independent of evidence semantics. During planning, every row invokes `renewRunLease` and then writes a checkpoint (`runWorkflow.ts:603–610`). `renewRunLease` updates both the shared lease and the run (`:1214–1247`), so planning alone normally performs three Mongo updates per row. During apply, `upsertActionReceipt` reads and rewrites the entire growing `receipts` array (`:1113–1138`). Proposed changes: time-budgeted lease renewal with enough expiry margin, combined bounded progress checkpoints, and atomic per-action receipt insertion/update or a separately bounded action result store. Preserve owner/epoch fencing, deterministic replay and recoverability after process death. Measure actual oplog bytes/row; do not equate update count with full-document disk bytes.

## 5. Archive write amplification and durable growth

`appendHistoryMonth` maintains one catalog pointer per job/month/page but republishes the **whole page** as immutable content on each append (`monthArchive.ts:38–96`). A page holds at most 512 chains and at most 8 MiB decoded BSON. The previous content-addressed object is not deleted when its pointer changes. The source contains no archive garbage collector. The Service says the history prefix is outside daily/weekly backup expiry rules.

The object count is therefore not simply `chains / 512`. With single-chain appends, a 512-chain page creates 512 progressively larger objects. Ignoring compression, the sum of serialized chain copies is 512 × 513 / 2 = 131,328, or 256.5 times the final 512-chain payload. This is a worst-case structural illustration, **not a measured amplification factor**; batches can append several chains and gzip benefits repeated values. Existing duplicates are recognized and do not create new content, but still cause object reads.

At 115,000 new archived chains/day, one-chain-per-publication could approach 115,000 object inserts/day plus verification reads, old-page reads, and separate closed-run uploads. Current code does an upload followed by a read in `storeArchiveObject`, then another read in `appendHistoryMonth`; closure also re-reads and re-verifies. Actual object counts, compressed sizes, request billing, class/location, lifecycle, soft deletion/versioning and current orphan bytes have not been measured here. Do not claim a dollar saving from moving data out of Atlas until those are inspected.

Recommended archive v2:

- Publish immutable bounded chunks of *new* chains instead of rewriting the prior page, grouped by exact job and time range. Keep a durable batch manifest/checksum and unique chunk membership so retries cannot duplicate logical evidence.
- Upload and verify once through a module returning a verified handle; retain a second independent verification only where the safety contract needs it. Avoid accidentally removing a safety check merely to reduce calls.
- Keep Mongo catalogs bounded by choosing size/time thresholds (for example job/day staging and periodic immutable month consolidation), while making partial days readable. Low-volume jobs should not create an object for every single capture.
- Consolidation publishes and verifies the replacement plus an atomic versioned catalog switch. Old objects become reclaimable only after reader grace, manifest reachability audit and rollback window. Do not delete objects still referenced by closed-run indexes, old catalogs, deletion manifests, or active readers.
- Inventory unreferenced v1 objects using a complete paginated object listing and catalog/closure reachability set. First deliver a dry-run report; perform no prefix-wide deletion. Define explicit retained archive duration with the Owner; current evidence-preservation semantics imply durable history, not automatic time expiry.
- Separate archive staging from the short Mongo certification transaction. Archive failure must leave source rows untouched and retryable. The final transaction still reloads exact source, references, winner and lease fence before stamping.

Forecast both systems: `Mongo hot bytes = eligible intake × hot days × measured physical bytes/chain + protected/durable growth`; `archive bytes = unique chains × compressed bytes/chain + closed-run replay bytes + catalog overhead + retained superseded objects`. Add request counts and restore read cost. Persistent archive catalog and closure collections need their own measured growth budget.

## 6. Recommended throughput specification

Introduce a versioned Mongo retention configuration, retaining the original immutable `new_data_from`, with pause/mode, bounded page size, pages per wake-up, time budget, archive concurrency, maximum in-flight bytes, retry policy and backlog-drain budget. No new environment flags. Record configuration changes as owner/operator decisions.

Use renewable owner-fenced leases and small independent pages. Network work stays outside Mongo transactions. Start with current 64-chain transaction granularity and process multiple pages per bounded worker invocation; do not jump straight to thousands of chains in one transaction. Give expensive/ineligible classes explicit next-review reasons and avoid daily rediscovery of permanent exclusions. A later architecture can use per-partition claims, but do not introduce parallel retention until archive catalog contention and ownership tests pass.

At the prior intake, target a sustained **qualified expiry** rate of at least 2× measured eligible arrival rate during backlog catch-up, subject to production latency, replication and disk-I/O guardrails. Illustratively, four 64-chain pages per two-minute tick produce 184,320 reviews/day at 100% availability and eligibility—only 1.60× 115,000, and less after held pages. Eight pages produce 368,640 nominal reviews/day. These are arithmetic budgets for rehearsal, not validated operating settings. Required pages derive from `arrival / (ticks × page_size × observed_unique_eligible_yield × availability)` plus a measured catch-up allowance.

Maintain a stable query access path for due work. First measure the existing sparse review index against the fixed date fences; then test a revised compound/partial index only if explain/latency proves it necessary. Preserve unique operation IDs, observation-per-receipt and decision-attempt integrity indexes. Do not remove provenance indexes on the basis of one quiet `$indexStats` sample.

Pause gates: increased business API latency above agreed baseline, growing oldest processing age, archive integrity error, Pool parity mismatch, replication lag/I/O threshold breach, lease overlap, or history/replay mismatch. A pause prevents new stamps; it does **not** resurrect TTL-deleted rows. Restoring previously removed evidence requires a separately rehearsed archive restoration procedure.

## 7. No-op compaction contract: preserve evidence before collapsing it

Preferred first phase: retain the current canonical lifecycle and make its seven-day lossless retention sustainable. This reduces long-term Mongo accumulation without changing “each fresh observation exists” semantics. Remove unrelated no-op fan-out and avoid repeated large run snapshot writes where proven; these are independent of observation meaning.

For the second phase, choose explicitly between two different contracts:

| Option | Preserved | Changed | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lossless representation compaction | Every operation ID, timestamp, observation ID, decision result and immutable normalized statement | Storage representation/read adapter | Preferred next prototype. Share repeated payload blobs and store compact per-operation acceptance records or batch records. |
| Count/last-seen aggregation | Latest state and a count | Exact per-observation times, original decision history and potentially replay | Not the default. Requires an explicit Owner-approved loss-of-detail contract. |
| Raw-row equality skip before lifecycle | Raw values look unchanged | Can skip temporal winner acceptance and later permit stale state; policy/identity changes also missed | Reject under current contract. |

The last-seen/count example in the earlier brief is insufficient by itself. A newer unchanged observation at 10:00 can protect against a delayed changed observation captured at 09:00. If 10:00 is skipped and the winner stays at 08:00, the 09:00 update may wrongly apply. Hash equality across observations also says nothing about changed Registry policy, target identity, source scope or canonicalization version.

Proposed lossless v2 boundary, `GranotEvidenceStore` (module name illustrative): own receipt acceptance, immutable normalized statement storage, exact operation replay, temporal acceptance evidence, archive lookup and hot/cold reconstruction. Existing planner and domain commands receive provider-neutral evidence contracts; callers never decide persistence shortcuts. The current Granot temporal tuple remains `(captured_at, observation_id)` with the same tie-breaker. Allocate each observation identity once even when its statement bytes are shared.

Acceptance must remain durable before acknowledging capture. Every operation stores exact hash/kind/result and versioned source/policy/target meaning. Equal-valued accepted observations atomically advance the winner together with durable acceptance evidence. Stale observations record their own outcome but never advance the winner. Business writes, official Bookings/Cancellations, ambiguity, conflicts, effects and webhooks retain full chains initially. Certified current evidence and protected references remain available without relying on an unverified cold fetch. Replays after hot expiry must return the original result and reject operation-ID/hash conflicts.

Do not mutate old append-only Decisions into counters. Introduce versioned records/read adapters and dual-read compatibility. An archive-backed full chain can be reconstructed deterministically only if all exact fields required by existing history, replay and provenance consumers are retained. A new representation does not justify synthetic historical observations.

Proposed rollout: compute compact candidate in shadow while still storing full chain; compare ordering, decisions, effects, history, replay and freshness; exercise mixed-version reads; only then owner-gated write mode for a closed eligible class. Rollback stops new v2 writes but readers must continue supporting already committed v2 evidence. Full rollback requires a tested lossless materializer, not a binary revert.

## 8. Action order and proof

1. **Correct the evidence baseline.** Fresh live policy/index/age distributions; exact incoming daily channel/outcome/effect counts; job-stage attribution; retained logical/physical/index bytes; archive object inventory; correlate failed batches to logs. Explain the November 8–9 ramp using live slot/run counts and deployment history before treating the release date as the whole explanation.
2. **Fix false business changes and consider the optional Owner stopgap.** Correct and qualify the missing Call `move_date` projection first in the implementation plan. Independently, the Owner may restore 08:00, 13:00, 18:00 admission in the shared schedule only, preserving independent 06:00 discovery, old manifests, pending runs, operation IDs and readers. The nominal slot reduction is 83.3%; measured receipt/disk reduction will differ with older dates, Pool and unrelated traffic. Freshness becomes up to five daytime hours and fourteen overnight hours between nominal slots. This is an explicit service tradeoff, not a silent engineering optimization. Existing in-flight work continues draining. Re-enable the 18-slot schedule to roll back after capacity qualification.
3. **Repair retention observability and the measured reference-read bottleneck, then lift throughput in bounded pages.** Reproduce failure classes on the disposable replica, implement bounded workers, prove exact history/replay and preserve cutoff. A complete archive v2 rewrite is not a prerequisite to a safe bounded v1 throughput release: measure object/network amplification and set byte/request budgets, then deliver archive v2 independently if v1 meets the short-term budget. Do not promote while unexplained failures remain without an explicit Owner exception and a documented residual-risk limit; never bypass checksum, replay, reference or ordering safety checks.
4. **Promote future retention only after qualification or an explicit documented gate override.** Keep every per-chain certification. Start throttled; verify actual TTL removal, rising reusable Mongo pages and stable workload. Retain seven-day shadow evidence where feasible; an override changes timing, not safety checks.
5. **Historical cleanup:** independently enumerate, archive, verify and manifest eligible before-cutoff chains and terminal bulky run payloads. Recompute every selected chain just before authorized removal. No manual Decision deletion outside that protocol.
6. **Compaction v2:** prototype shared statements/compact per-operation acceptances; get the semantic contract approved; deploy only after mixed-version and ordering qualification.
7. **Physical space reclamation:** evaluate after sustained traffic/retention equilibrium and after purge oplog churn subsides. Deleting Mongo documents generally creates reusable space; it does not itself reduce provisioned Atlas disk. Parent mission owns the current Atlas-supported compact/failover details and billing model. Do not forecast reduced tier cost from deleted logical bytes alone.

Replica acceptance suite must cover: identical replay before/after hot removal; same operation with changed hash/kind conflict; fresh equal snapshot then delayed changed snapshot; equal-time ObjectId ordering; concurrent accepted observations/CAS loser; policy and target changes with identical raw payload; Form/Call sibling behavior; current/provenance/Pool/case references; exact Lead History and Job Timeline hot/cold parity; archived alias identity; closed-run absent/present/missing/unfinished/>5,000 actions; archive timeout and checksum failure; transaction/lease expiry; process death between upload, catalog publish and stamp; repeated sweep idempotency; cutoff fences; shadow repeated counts; TTL lag; mixed-version rollback. Load rehearsal should run realistic 18-slot batches with approximately 115,000 arrivals/day equivalent, measure sustained eligible expiry rate and peak transaction/network work, and prove backlog falls while business latency stays acceptable.

No new runtime behavior has been implemented or qualified in this review. The code findings make the implementation order reviewable; remaining proof is the live failure diagnosis, measured archive economics, replica behavior/load results, and controlled production qualification.
