## How retries work

| Situation | What the server does today | Rule for moving data out of Mongo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Granot sends a webhook | Commit the redacted receipt before returning 202. Failed persistence returns 503. A queue message speeds up processing. A five-minute cron can find the receipt if the message is lost. Identical incoming deliveries get distinct receipt IDs. | Keep durable capture ahead of acknowledgement. Count each accepted delivery exactly once even if two workers see it. Repeated delivery cannot duplicate a canonical business effect. |
| A worker crashes or encounters a technical error | A five-minute lease allows takeover. Completion checks the lease owner. Technical attempts 1–9 back off from 30 seconds, doubling with a six-hour cap before jitter. The tenth consecutive failure becomes a dead letter. An Owner can requeue it with a recorded reason. | Keep the payload, lease, next attempt and error while actionable. A missing archive or failed upload must retry archive work independently and must never re-run a Lead change or a provider send. |
| Granot arrives before the matching Lead | The business retry clock follows capture time, with checks at 1, 5 and 15 minutes, then 1, 2, 6, 12 and 24 hours. At the end of the window the processor can record an unmatched decision. | Pending matches stay hot. A terminal unmatched result may still feed a review or reconciliation case. Materialize that case evidence before releasing its transport copy. |
| Scheduled automation or the extension retries an operation | Same channel, operation ID, kind and payload hash return the original result. Changed kind or content conflicts (409). A scheduled operation is identified by run and action. Each new scheduled action must still pass its original approval deadline, currently 24 hours from run creation. | Return the same receipt, observation, decision, target and effects after archiving. Keep the extension's own identity namespace. A replay can return a recorded result after expiry. Expiry cannot authorize another action. |
| A Sales Intelligence job fails | A scoped job key absorbs duplicate nominations. A lease and epoch fence effects. Jobs normally allow eight attempts, schema-invalid work stops after two, and provider throttling defers without spending an attempt. | Pending, leased, retrying, paused, dead-letter and unresolved provider work stays available to its worker/operator. Retiring a completed row must also preserve any replay, repair or admissions dependency. |

The capture and drain contracts live in [Granot capture](../knowledge/granot-lifecycle/capture.md) and [claim, retry and Owner requeue](../knowledge/granot-lifecycle/drainer.md). Source baseline `615ff367` reached the production alias at 1:47:15 p.m. The census started 31 seconds later and includes accumulated stock from earlier releases. That baseline already includes five scheduled slots and combined receipt completion and winning Lead certification. [Production qualification](GRANOT-HOT-STORAGE-2026-11-18.md)

### 3. Archive completed evidence and bulky runs as part of normal processing

Build on `granotHistory` and the existing private GCS bucket. The processor commits its business result and required follow-up intent in Mongo. A separate bounded worker seals completed evidence, uploads immutable compressed batches, verifies generation, length, SHA-256, decoded counts and sample reconstruction, then publishes the catalog pointer under a compare-and-set. Reuse `granot_history_archive_chunks` by job and `granot_closed_automation_runs` by run. Store the original redacted BSON and operation results. A receipt with no usable job number needs a locator discoverable by its receipt ID. Batch those locators and price them before extending coverage. Isolate recovery access from the routine dashboard.

**First cohort.** Target completed, superseded, effect-free scheduled HTTP Lead snapshots (`already_current` or a proven older `stale`). Try closure at the original action deadline when all sealed actions and downstream nominations have settled. Keep current winners, protected references, active cases, partial runs, unresolved failures and mixed/legacy shapes hot until their specific independent read and recovery proofs pass. Current code waits for the hot run to disappear and enforces a seven-day cutoff. In the 24–25-hour sample, all 63 routine results had a newer winner, yet all seven original runs still existed and none had a closure pointer. Re-run under the five-slot schedule. This is the first storage engineering blocker. [Age and closure evidence](evidence/granot-early-closure-sample-2026-11-18.json)

**Run closure.** Publish the full terminal run, sealed plan/checksum, original expiry, per-action IDs/hashes/results and diagnostic details. Compare sealed action membership to committed results, including explicitly held/skipped rows. Retain a small terminal run/slot record with coverage, tallies, failure summary and locator. It must satisfy deterministic child upserts and recovery while any slot can dispatch that identity. Otherwise an eight-day slot can recreate a child whose seven-day run has aged out. A terminal error stays actionable until resolved or explicitly dispositioned. Change the current unconditional run `purge_at` behavior for the new format before relying on verified closure. An archive outage may delay retirement, never extend execution authority.

**Safe removal and replay.** Until publication commits, Mongo serves current state. After publication, serve exact legacy or archived operation results and reject conflicting payloads. Recheck the winner, action closure, pending work, protected holders and exact source/hash in a bounded transaction before removal. Writers that could acquire a new reference must share the fence or atomically pin independent evidence. A momentary absence from a reference query is insufficient. Retry an unreferenced upload, lost pointer race, interrupted delete or failed verification by the same identity. Missing/corrupt objects hold deletion and raise an actionable error. Retain restore manifests and readers for both formats across rollback. Limit concurrent reads/writes and track the oldest unpublished result so a GCS outage cannot silently fill the volume.

**Next cohorts.** Archive true webhooks once the daily buckets and case/command evidence stand alone. Extend separately to effect-bearing decisions and extension operations after exact replay, 409 conflict, original provenance, redelivery and failure/requeue proofs. Extension IDs have no scheduled run to look up. Keep a compact channel/operation key, kind, hash and result/locator for that contract. Test each cohort with the full raw chain absent. For sustained write reduction, then prototype storing routine scheduled normalization and decision together on the active receipt, with stable observation/decision IDs and exact replay. Compare real insert/update counts and bytes before promoting it. Ambiguous, pending and business-changing outcomes remain on their proven path during this experiment.

### 5. Remove retired collections and return to the optimization program

1. Use an exact namespace manifest for feature-owned collections. Export and restore those rows, indexes and necessary private recovery material in isolation. Inventory shared collections separately. Restrict historical Granot cleanup to archive-verified cohorts.
2. Verify the compatible server/Admin release, stopped crons and subscriptions, removed or fenced manual executors, zero remaining live callers and boot-time model imports. After the verified rollback window, drop the exclusive namespaces. Check for recreation after a cold start, a scheduler cycle and the next daily janitor window. A rollback before removal can restore code. After removal, restoring the prior feature also requires its data and indexes.
3. Compare a full five-slot day including overnight recovery, the busy hour and twice the forecast. At 64 records every two minutes and 80% unique utilization, one page yields **36,864 unique advances/day** versus **26,721/day** at 75% count eligibility. At twice the volume two pages provide the same relative margin. Count actual verified unique removals, retries, holds, catalogue/run summaries and pending bytes. Measure cold GET count, compressed bytes, p95 lookup/restore time, GCS operations/egress and per-member free/reusable bytes. An archive outage drill must recover its backlog while new work arrives. Cost claims use measured bills.
4. Accept the change when eligible backlog drains faster than arrivals until caught up, the steady hot set stays within the measured budget, every retired workflow/namespace stays absent, retained views and both master sheets pass parity, and exact replay or restore works after removal. Keep Sheet S6, Granot's separate performance qualification and existing shadow-retention promotion checks visible. Prior cleanup authority applied to its exact manifests.
5. Use that evidence to re-rank the remaining services. Inventory each `src/services/` candidate with its route, worker, cron, clients, collections and last observed use. Remove only independently proved unused services and their exclusive collections. Set the next roadmap or write a successor using the observed bottleneck.
